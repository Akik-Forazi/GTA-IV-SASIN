# The Accountant

**The hidden stage-manager.** Status refs: canon-ledger 2.9, 4.3, 4.5, 4.10; chronology B3, C2–C4. Identity withheld from the player until Act IV. PROPOSED identity: **Elias Marsh**, 57 — the network's comptroller and records-keeper; a former Big Four audit partner who built Baek's payment rails in the 1990s and never left.

## Player-facing definition (by act)
- **Act I**: a presence, not a person: the "—A." signature, the voice-changed call, the choreography of nudges. The player should feel *authorship* without seeing an author.
- **Act II**: a shape: every fabrication the group unearths has clean bookkeeping — someone *keeps* the lies as carefully as the truth.
- **Act III**: the deduction (Min-ji's, M11): "Every fabrication needs a bookkeeper." The staged job, the light coffin, the kept-alive witness — all one signature. The player now knows the Accountant exists; not who.
- **Act IV**: the face (M12–M13): Marsh, met at a distance — mild, tidy, the only man in the conspiracy who dresses like what he is. His defection (M14) delivers the master ledger's location — and the location is *also* bait, because the Accountant's survival has always depended on there being one move left.
- **Act V**: witness for the exposure package; his records make Min-ji's detonation airtight. He does not ask for money. He asks for **immunity and boredom** — "I would like, for the rest of my life, to be unremarkable." Epilogue: a small-town bookkeeping practice under his real name, watched forever.

## What he actually did (writer-only spine) **[P-4.3, P-4.5]**
- **Staged the massacre's pretext**: forged the letter of authority that deceived Jae-Ho; wrote Kostov's job ticket; positioned the liaison; falsified the after-action ("heirs unlocated, records believed destroyed") — the two lies that created the entire campaign.
- **Kept Jae-Ho alive** as insurance against the day Baek tidies *him*.
- **2025**: saw the trust notice before anyone; understood Baek's succession war had reached the ledger; and made the only move that keeps him alive past the war — **aim the son at the chairman**. The prints, the text, the call, the docks meet, the torn page: all one hand.
- **His rule of self**: he has never once touched the money. He touches only *records of* money. It is the superstition that keeps him honest to both sides — and it is the hook Min-ji pulls (M14): she offers him what Baek never would — a record of his own, sealed, read only when he is dead.

## Voice
- Quiet, exact, faintly amused by professionalism wherever he finds it ("Your sister's memo structure is very good; I improved it twice"). Never threatens; states consequences. On the phone he is someone else; in person he is nobody — the most dangerous voice in the campaign is the mildest.

## Knowledge boundaries
- Knows: everything about the 2011 staging; the ledger's contents summary; Baek's succession map; the trust's mechanics.
- Does **not** know: whether the trust has copies he hasn't seen (it does — the engine of M15), what Haru will do when offered control (nobody does), or that Min-ji has been assembling her package since M6 (his one fatal blind spot: he prices enemies, not *auditors*).

## Relationships
- **Baek**: employee who knows too much; the dependency is mutual and poisonous.
- **Jae-Ho**: his insurance; he thinks of him as a *held position*, never a person — until M11, when the confession he overhears (via the watchers' feed) makes him feel the cost in a way his ledger never recorded.
- **Min-ji**: the opponent he never planned for. Their one direct scene (M14's defection) is the campaign's coldest, truest negotiation: two record-keepers deciding whose truth outranks.

## Continuity guards
- Never appears on-screen or by name before Act IV; Act I–III presence is artifacts and voice only.
- He never confesses the whole conspiracy in one speech (canon 2.10 prohibition); every fact he gives is priced, partial, and checkable against evidence the player has already seen.
- He is not the mastermind: he is the instrument that made the mastermind's order executable, and the reason it is provable.
