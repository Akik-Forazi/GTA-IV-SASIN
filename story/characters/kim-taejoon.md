# Kim Tae-joon ("Chairman Kim")

**The adoptive father. Deceased.** Status refs: canon-ledger 1.9, 2.6, 4.1–4.2; chronology A2–B2. 1948–2011. PROPOSED: name.

## The binding rule (canon 1.9)
He is **neither completely innocent nor completely evil**, and never retroactively made stupid for a twist. Everything below honors that line. He is the campaign's hardest problem: a good father who ran dirty rails for fifteen years, and whose last good act summoned the massacre.

## Life
- Busan dockworker's son; Baek Seung-ho's protégé from the Haeun shipping desks of the 1970s; built Seorin Group in Liberty City from 1988 — logistics, warehousing, marine services.
- 1994: adopts Ahsan (Haru, age 8) with his wife. The adoption was love with mixed motives he never separated — the wife's longing, the merger optics of an heir, and the ache of a man who made his money invisible and wanted one visible thing.
- Mid-1990s: the network's money begins riding Seorin's rails. He knows the margins are dirty. He tells himself the installments lie: *fees, not freight; rounding, not routing.* Fifteen years of not itemizing **[P-4.1]**.

## What he knew, and when
- **1994–2010**: knows the money is gray; assumes "gray" means laundering and influence, the sins of his class. Does not look harder because the family lives well and the employees eat.
- **Late 2010**: the audit flag Min-ji almost catches. He follows it alone. He learns "cleanup" means **eliminations** — and that at least two closed jobs ride dates and warehouses he recognizes. He does not tell his family (shame, fear) and does not go to the authorities (he no longer trusts what the network owns). He begins cutting Seorin out, quietly, account by account **[P-4.1]**.
- **February 2011**: two acts, days apart — he amends his will to place the **master ledger** (his private, contemporaneous record of every network transaction Seorin carried, kept fifteen years as life insurance) into a trust unlocking on the tenth anniversary of his death, to his children jointly; and he initiates the **final transaction**: a buyout of Seorin's obligations, routed — fatally — through the only house he still trusted at that altitude: **Haeun** **[P-4.2]**.
- **March 9, 2011**: the warehouse. He is home. The network's visitors arrive separately; the medical examiner writes "cardiac event." It is murder; it is deniable; no one is ever charged.

## The verdict the campaign renders
Not exoneration, not indictment — **accounting**. The ledger itself is the verdict: fifteen years of entries in his own hand, with dates, margins, and the two jobs in 2010 he underlined and tried to refuse. Haru reads it (M15) and must hold both truths: the man who signed the rails and the man who spent his last months trying to pull the family out — and whose exit route is what killed them all. The flaw the son inherited (control as certainty) is the father's exact mistake at national scale: Tae-joon was *certain* Haeun would honor the deal because Baek had never once failed to honor a price.

## Voice (flashbacks, recordings, letters only — he never appears "alive" on-screen)
- Warm, wry, formal with his children and unformal in letters. The recordings the trust delivers (M15) are spoken, not written: a father who understood the medium his children would need. He never excuses himself in them; he never performs guilt either. He *accounts*.

## Writer-only notes
- The ledger's fifteen years of entries include two entries where he **refused payment** (2010) — the proof of the turn. The player sees the physical pages; the dates are checkable against the network's own records later.
- His one contact with Niko survives in a 2011 expense note: "N. Bellic — discretion, above rate, worth it." The first document Haru ever sees with both men's names on it (M5).
- Never retcon him into secret omniscience: he never knew the Beneficiary's identity — he paid the house, not the man. That single misunderstanding is the tragedy's hinge.

## Continuity guards
- He never appears alive in any flashback timeline; all his presence is documents, recordings, memory, and the trust.
- No scene may imply he knew of the massacre in advance or colluded in his own death.
- The wife (Haru's adoptive mother) died of illness in 2006 — natural death, no conspiracy, and the campaign never contradicts it.
