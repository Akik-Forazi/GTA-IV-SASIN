# Jae-Ho Lee (이재호)

**"The Man Who Lied."** Status refs: canon-ledger 2.7, 4.4–4.5, 5.3; chronology B3–B6. Age 61 in 2019. PROPOSED: role definition (head of security & the father's fixer).

## Player-facing definition (what the player may conclude by act)
- **Act I**: the family's head of security; officially "died protecting the family" in 2009; his grave is a plot device that refuses to stay quiet.
- **Act II–III**: alive; hidden; watched; kept on crumbs. The question stops being *where is he* and becomes *what did he do*.
- **Act III (M10 confession)**: the truth, in his voice, unvarnished.
- **Act IV–V**: leveraged by the network; protected at cost; lives to face the family.

## Voice
- Formal, old-school, self-flagellating in a controlled way. Speaks of himself in the third person when ashamed ("Jae-Ho opened the doors. Jae-Ho will close them."). Refuses softening language for his own acts; uses it compulsively for everyone else's.

## What he knowingly did vs. what he was deceived about (binding) **[P-4.4]**
- **Knowingly (before)**: he set the warehouse transfer schedule, stood down two camera positions, and booked the "retrieval specialists" through Kostov's broker — on the written instruction of his employer *the family*, backed by a letter of authority he checked and found genuine. His deception was built on a real document his own chain produced.
- **Deceived about**: the existence of any hostile party; the meaning of "with prejudice" (he read it as legal boilerplate); the fact that the letter was forged by the Accountant on the Beneficiary's instruction.
- **The night**: four minutes of paralysis when the radios turned to screaming — then the warning call to the loading office that moved Haru and Min-ji into Niko's path. His verdict on himself, verbatim, M10: *"Courage that arrives late is still a guest, not a host. But it arrives."*
- **After**: he believed the network would hunt him; the Accountant's people staged his death *at* him instead — he understands only in 2019 (M11) that his survival was never mercy and never his own cleverness. It was filing.

## Why he survived (writer-only) **[P-4.5]**
The Accountant's insurance: a live witness against the night Baek decided to tidy his ledger. Jae-Ho is a *held* asset — fed, watched, harmless — which is why the grave crumbs and the watchers orbit him without touching.

## Arc by act
- **I**: present as absence — the grave, the light coffin (M2), the apartment that was searched before Haru got there.
- **II**: his paper trail (the courier note with Kostov's name) is the act's spine; the sexton and the gravedigger fill M9's ledger.
- **III — The Man Who Lied** (M9–M11): found; confession delivered on his knees to no one in particular; the rupture it triggers with Niko (who half-knew, half-hoped he was dead) and with Haru (who owes him his life and his family's deaths in the same breath). Act ends with his repossession by the network — the doomsday file called in.
- **IV**: leveraged, not broken: he feeds the network truth that is *old*, buying days. The counterstroke (M14) uses him as bait; Niko's tradecraft and Mandu's bluff pull him out — the campaign's best action set-piece, and the moment Niko chooses to save the man he blamed.
- **V**: faces Min-ji and Haru without defense; makes his statement to the exposure package as a *witness*, not a defendant; declines to run afterward. Epilogue: he tends the Bohan churchyard — his choice, accepted by the family, watched by a city that no longer hunts him.

## Relationships
- **Tae-joon**: the master he failed — and the reason he cannot die quietly: the ledger was *Tae-joon's* answer, and Jae-Ho is its last living context.
- **Haru / Min-ji**: the debt with no currency. He expects nothing; the family's verdict (M15) is the act's emotional pivot.
- **Niko**: colleagues who never compared notes — each believed the other had died of the same night. Their M11 scene is two accounting errors meeting.
- **Mandu**: the con artist is the only one who speaks to him like a person; the sexton thread makes Mandu his first visitor in a decade.

## Continuity guards
- Headstone: "JAE-HO LEE — 2009 — HE DIED PROTECTING THE FAMILY" (2009 per P-3.1; the epitaph is the Accountant's paperwork, not the family's belief).
- He never met the Beneficiary, never heard the title, and cannot name the Accountant — he knows the forged letter's signature block, which is M12's evidence.
- He does not know the ledger's location; he knows it *exists* (Tae-joon's life-insurance habit) — that testimony is what makes the trust (M15) credible to the player.
