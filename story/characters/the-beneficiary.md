# The Beneficiary — Baek Seung-ho ("the Chairman")

**The top of the chain.** Status refs: canon-ledger 1.11, 4.11–4.13; chronology A1, C2. Age 79 in 2025. **PROPOSED — HIGH IMPACT: identity requires explicit user approval (Decision Queue #1).**

## Player-facing definition (by act)
- **Act I**: unnamed. The word "beneficiary" does not exist in the player's vocabulary yet; the player feels only that the Russians had a *buyer*.
- **Act II (M6)**: Min-ji's analysis coins **"the Beneficiary"** as a structural fact — the account at the end of every dirty trail that only ever *receives*. A title earned by evidence, not announced by a villain (canon 4.10; ledger 7.4 REJECTED the Act I name-drop).
- **Act III**: a presence in the succession war's rumors: an old man whose house is deciding who inherits the century.
- **Act IV (M12–M13)**: named and met: **Baek Seung-ho**, honorary chairman of the Haeun Group, Tae-joon's mentor, the diaspora's gray eminence. The parley.
- **Act V**: destroyed — by his own arithmetic. Exposure detonates; his partners, given *certainty*, remove him. Haru, in the one room where the kill is easy, declines it — and that refusal, to a man who prices everything, is the sentence he cannot serve.

## The man
- Built Haeun from a Busan shipping trust into a trans-pacific web. Publicly: honors, boards, a scholarship foundation. Privately: he owns almost nothing and is owed almost everything — obligations, not assets; **entitlement as an art form** **[A1]**.
- His instrument was the network: payments, logistics, cleanup — routed through other men's warehouses so no fingerprint ever touched him. Kim Tae-joon was his best rail, and the closest thing to a son the system allowed. When the rail tried to exit through the front door of Haeun itself, he priced it: the ledger was worth more than the man **[B2]**.
- 2025: old, mid-**succession war**, aware the house is sniffing his age. The trust notice is the one variable he cannot buy, delay, or bury: it *unlocks by law*. His need is the clock the whole act runs on.

## Method (anti-omnipotence rules — canon 4.13)
- He never personally orders anything traceable; he **sets conditions** — officials, intermediaries, deniable assets — and lets systems act. He does not kill Haru in Act I because killing the son before the ledger surfaces risks burying the one map to it. He *watches* instead. His patience is a constraint, not a power.
- He is mortal and beatable: his reach in LC is real but rented; his succession war means his own lieutenants are candidates, not assets; and every asset he owns can be re-priced by a better offer — which is exactly what Min-ji's package does to them in M16.

## Blind spots (canon 4.12 — binding for the ending)
1. **He prices everyone in fear and greed** — so a con artist who declines an offer (M16) is not a variable he has; the crack in his composure is the campaign's quietest climax.
2. **He cannot model exposure chosen over leverage** — the package Min-ji builds from M6 is, to him, a negotiating asset; he keeps trying to *buy* it during M16 because he literally cannot process that it is not for sale.
3. **He cannot model control relinquished** — his offer to Haru (M13: the network's chair, priced in silence) assumes the son's flaw is a lever. The offer works exactly once, at Haru's worst hour — and its collapse is what teaches Haru the difference between control and certainty.

## Voice
- Generational courtesy over steel; never raises his voice because he has never needed to. Speaks in prices and instruments ("You have an asset I need; I have certainty you need"). His courtesy never wavers, including at the end — which is what makes the ending land.

## The parley (M13) — the scene the whole campaign aims at until now
- Neutral ground (Haeun's LC offices, night). He offers Haru: the network's LC chair, Seorin's debts dissolved, the past filed under "unresolved." The price: the ledger page, the burner, and the siblings' silence. He makes the offer the way a man sets a table. Haru — the flaw's high-water mark — *almost* takes it. What stops him is Min-ji's voice in his ear: her doctrine, inverted — *"He isn't offering you control. He's pricing your silence. Count what silence costs the people who died holding it."*
- The parley ends without violence: Baek does not do violence; he does *schedules*. The counterstroke (M14) is already booked.

## The Reckoning (M16–M17) — how he falls **[P-5.5]**
- Min-ji's package (records + Kostov's testimony + Marsh's ledgers + the trust) is delivered to the people who can act on it: regulators, the succession war's factions, and one very careful journalist.
- The partners remove Baek — the succession war eats its king — because with certainty of exposure, the *priced* outcome changes: his certainty is worth more to them as a sacrifice than as a chairman.
- The final room (M17, the harbor offices at the docks where "—A." first texted): Haru holds the kill and declines it. **"I don't need you dead. I need you certain you've lost."** For a man whose entire self is pricing, that sentence is the execution.
- Epilogue: his house survives, smaller, governed; his name outlives him in filings, not foundations. He never learns which record-keeper he failed to price — and that is Min-ji's victory, not Haru's kill.

## Continuity guards
- Never appears before M12 on-screen; Act I–III references are structural ("the account," "the client's buyer") never personal.
- He never meets Jae-Ho, never speaks to Niko, never learns the Accountant defected until it is filed.
- He is never shown doing violence, never shown panicking; his ruin is procedural and that is the point.
