# Roman Bellic

**Family node; earned agency.** Status refs: canon-ledger 1.1, 2.2; chronology C4, Part D M8. GTA IV canon character — Revenge ending: **alive**, married to Mallorie, father of a young daughter (named Kate, per P-1.18).

## Player-facing definition
- Runs the taxi and livery business — bigger than in 2008, leveraged to match: depot payroll, medallion loans, and the corporate accounts he took to cover the mortgage. Prosperous enough to be a target, ordinary enough to be reachable.
- The campaign's structural vulnerability **and** its proof that vulnerability is not helplessness (canon-ledger 2.2 — confirmed).

## Voice
- Fast, warm, bargaining even when bargaining is pointless ("I give you the friend rate, which is still a rate"). Babbles when frightened — and *knows* he babbles, which he turns into material. His courage is louder than it is large and shows up exactly when it counts.

## Flaw
Optimistic leverage: he grew the business on debt and trust in the wrong clients' paperwork. He signs things. In 2025 he has signed a corporate account he cannot read his way out of — which is how the network rides his depot (M8's discovery), and why his vulnerability is structural, not personal stupidity.

## The earned moment (M8 — binding)
Roman notices the pattern himself: bookings of his cabs cluster in a rotation no dispatcher set — the same six blocks, the same shift gaps, invoices paid from a shell he never heard of. **Roman brings the pattern to Haru; Haru did not find it.** This is the character beat that makes him a partner in the plot instead of a hostage in it, and it must never be given to Niko or Haru instead.

## Arc by act
- **I**: host and heart. The safehouse dinners, the friend-rate jokes, the fear he masks with hospitality. He is the first to say out loud (M3) "they watched *my* depot, didn't they?" — he is not oblivious; he is practicing bravery.
- **II**: the pattern (M8). His discovery opens the watchers' rotation and their safehouse. He insists on driving the decoy route himself — his depot, his men, his risk — and Niko lets him, which is its own character beat.
- **III**: the scare that isn't staged for plot: a fake booking call lures a depot driver (not Roman) into a bad corner. Roman's response is not to hide but to lock the books down and give Haru the ledger copies — agency under fear.
- **IV**: Baek's counterstroke (M14) hits the depot; Mallorie and the kids are moved to Mallorie's cousin's place in Algonquin; Roman refuses to leave the city ("my cabs are how your witnesses move, cousin") — his business becomes the campaign's safe logistics.
- **V**: he drives the night of the reckoning — ordinary man, extraordinary steadiness. Epilogue: the depot turns a real profit for the first time since 2011, and he cries about it at dinner, and it is the funniest and most moving scene in the act.

## Relationships
- **Niko**: the debt runs both ways now — Niko protected Roman's family by staying silent; Roman's family is what Niko protects by standing.
- **Haru**: "cousin" — chosen family, without irony. Roman is the only one allowed to tease the reaper about eating properly.
- **Mallorie + daughter**: the non-negotiable. Every plan Haru builds from M3 onward carries their evacuation line item; Min-ji audits it.
- **Kate**: Roman carries his own grief gently and talks about her more easily than Niko can — which lets the player breathe.

## Continuity guards
- Married to Mallorie; child present in scenes only as texture (bedtime, drawings on the depot wall), never in danger on-screen.
- Never reduced to a helpless plot device: every "Roman in danger" beat must show a decision he makes, not just a trap he is in.
- Past tense for Kate; no Deal-ending implications anywhere in his dialogue.
