## PART A — THE DEEP PAST (1970s–2008)

### A1. Baek Seung-ho and the Haeun Group **[P-4.11]**
- Baek Seung-ho built the Haeun Group from a Busan shipping trust into a trans-pacific conglomerate with quiet operations in Liberty City. Publicly: philanthropy, boardrooms, honors. Privately: he learned early that ownership is clumsy and *entitlement is elegant* — he rarely owns anything; he owns the **obligations** of people who do.
- His instrument was the network later called **Operation Black Ledger**: a payment-and-service web that routes money, favors, weapons logistics, and "cleanup" through other people's legitimate infrastructure, leaving his hands clean and his exposure nil.
- The network's muscle was diaspora Russian ex-military talent brokered through fixers; its books were kept by a single comptroller known to the street only as **the Accountant** **[P-4.3]**.

### A2. Kim Tae-joon's rise and compromise **[P-2.6, P-4.1]**
- Kim Tae-joon, Baek's protégé from the Busan years, built **Seorin Group** **[P-1.17]** in Liberty City: legitimate logistics, shipping services, warehousing.
- From the mid-1990s, Seorin's rails carried network money. Tae-joon told himself the classic lie in installments: *fees, not freight; rounding errors, not routing* **[P-4.1]**. He became wealthy on margins he never asked to see itemized.
- 1994: Tae-joon and his wife adopt an eight-year-old orphan from Dhaka, **Ahsan**, through a church charity connection. He is raised as **Kim Haru** beside their daughter **Kim Min-ji** (b. 1984). The adoption is real love, entered for mixed reasons Tae-joon never fully disentangled — a fact that will matter to the ending.
- Jae-Ho Lee **[P-2.7]**, Tae-joon's right hand and head of security, runs the practical side of everything: route security, personnel, the awkward errands. He and Niko Bellic's world collide only later.

### A3. Niko's relevant history (canon-locked)
- 2008: the events of GTA IV. Niko works for and against Liberty City's criminal establishment; Kate McReary dies at Roman's wedding after Niko chooses revenge on Dimitri Rascalov; Roman and Mallorie survive, and Mallorie is pregnant at the epilogue. *(Revenge ending. This is fixed canon and may not be revised.)*
- Early 2009: hollowed out by Kate's death, needing work that asks nothing of his soul, Niko takes discreet private security contracts. Packie McReary refers him to a Korean businessman who "pays clean and asks quiet." Niko becomes part-time security for Kim Tae-joon. This is employment, not friendship — yet. **[P-3.1]**

## PART B — THE FALL (2008–2009) **[P-3.1]**

### B1. What Tae-joon discovered
- Late 2008, a routine internal audit Haru's sister-to-be Min-ji (then finishing university, already auditing for her father) flags a payment class she can't reconcile. Tae-joon follows it alone, off the books.
- He finds the "cleanup" ledger entries: the network's services include **extrajudicial eliminations** — witnesses, competitors, a journalist's informer — routed, priced, and closed out through *his* warehouses and *his* accounts.
- Tae-joon does not go to the police (he no longer trusts what the network owns) and does not tell his family (shame + fear). He begins, in careful increments, to **cut Seorin's rails out of the network**: closing shell accounts, refusing payment classes, letting leases lapse.

### B2. The final transaction — what made it worse **[P-4.2]**
- Cutting the rails quietly would take years, and Baek's people were already asking why freight was slowing. Tae-joon attempts the shortcut that damns him: he tries to **buy the family out** — a single negotiated payment to retire Seorin's obligations forever.
- He routes the approach through the only intermediary he still trusts at Baek's altitude: **the Haeun Group itself.**
- This does three catastrophic things: (1) it reveals to Baek that Tae-joon knows about the cleanup services; (2) it reveals Tae-joon believes the obligations *can* be retired — meaning he has kept **records**; (3) it puts a price discovery on the one thing Baek cannot allow to exist: the **master ledger** — Tae-joon's private, dated, contemporaneous record of every network transaction Seorin ever carried, kept for fifteen years as his life insurance **[P-4.6]**.
- Baek does not rage. He prices. He concludes the ledger is worth more than Tae-joon, and that the family's holdings, transferred to more compliant heirs, will survive a tragic loss. He instructs the Accountant to "close the Seorin exposure." The Accountant, who survives by anticipating his employer, stages the job.

### B3. The staged job **[P-4.3]**
- The Accountant fabricates a pretext with three layers, each tailored to its audience:
  - **To Jae-Ho**: a hostile party has stolen Seorin documents and plans to kidnap the family during a scheduled warehouse transfer of legacy records; a "retrieval with prejudice" crew must secure the site and the family's papers. Jae-Ho's job is access: doors, schedules, cameras off.
  - **To Kostov's crew**: a disputed asset transfer; the "family" on site are squatters in over their heads with a rival syndicate; loose hands authorized, paperwork to follow. Standard cleanup pricing.
  - **To the Beneficiary**: a clean termination report of the Seorin exposure — no mention of a staged pretext, no mention that the Accountant has quietly arranged the survival of exactly one witness.
- A **suited liaison** — the Accountant's emissary, not Baek — attends the operation to represent "the client" and to confirm completion. The crew's **spotter** photographs the operation per network insurance protocol. This photographer is the source of every photograph in the campaign.

### B4. The massacre — March 9, 2009 **[P-3.1, P-4.4]**
- Night, a Seorin transfer warehouse in Bohan's industrial edge. Kostov's crew executes the staff and security detail. Jae-Ho, running access from the office, realizes from the radio traffic that "retrieval" has become slaughter.
- He does the two things that define him: he **freeze-panics for four minutes** (his eternal shame), then he **warns the loading office** — where, not by luck, Tae-joon's children are: Haru (22) had come along that night to help inventory, and Min-ji had come to close the transfer paperwork. Jae-Ho's too-late call moves them toward the office's freight exit — into the path of the one armed man already heading there.
- Niko, on perimeter patrol, hears the shots and reaches the loading office first. He kills two of the crew in the corridor and takes Haru and Min-ji out through the freight cut. During the extraction, at the warehouse door, Niko passes the body of the **suited liaison** — shot by Kostov's own man in an early "loose hands" moment. Off the body, in the dark, Niko takes a **burner phone** **[P-4.7]**. He does not yet know why, except that it felt like the only piece of "why" that would ever be within reach.
- Eleven die. Tae-joon is not at the warehouse (Baek's men visit him separately, at home, the same night: a cardiac event, per the medical examiner. It is murder; it is deniable; no one will ever be charged). The children — now the "heirs" — are moved into protective obscurity abroad by the family attorneys within the week.

### B5. Why the children were spared — stated plainly **[P-4.4, P-4.14]**
- Not mercy. Not plan. Jae-Ho's warning moved them to the exit; Niko's speed closed the distance. The network's after-action (staged by the Accountant) records the deaths as "exposure terminated, heirs unlocated, records believed destroyed." The Accountant falsified the last two clauses. Both the children's survival and the ledger's survival are **his** fabrications — his insurance policy against the night Baek decides an Accountant who knows too much is itself an exposure.

### B6. Jae-Ho's disappearance **[P-4.5]**
- The Accountant cannot let Jae-Ho be buried in the same grave as the truth. He stages Jae-Ho's death: a body from the county morgue (ajohn Doe of similar build), a closed casket, a grieving-security-hero narrative the family attorneys accept because the alternative is asking questions they cannot afford.
- He then plants Jae-Ho in a long, quiet hiding: kept alive, kept silent, fed enough crumbs to feel safe, never enough to act. Officially to keep a witness "on ice." In truth, because a live Jae-Ho with a testimony is the Accountant's doomsday file against Baek.
- The grave in the Bohan churchyard reads: **JAE-HO LEE — 2009 — HE DIED PROTECTING THE FAMILY.** Both a lie and, in a four-minute-late way, a verdict.

### B7. Niko's silence (2009–2019) **[P-4.7, P-4.8]**
- Niko powers the burner phone on exactly once, in 2009, long enough to read one undelivered-broadcast text on it: **"ACCOUNT CLEARS FRIDAY. THEN THE BROOM."** Then he pulls the battery. He understands: the warehouse was a *contract*, and the contract is part of an account system — someone's tidy, recurring, priced business.
- He traces the account reference as far as a libraried phone book and two bad favors will go — far enough to learn the trail touches people who could reach Roman. Then Kate is dead (the wedding), and Niko decides the arithmetic of grief: he could not save Kate by learning everything; he will not risk Roman and the two survivors by trying.
- What Niko **witnessed**: the suited liaison at the door, directing, not shooting. What he **inferred**: professional contract job, not turf. What he **concealed**: the phone, the text, the description of the liaison. What he **does not know**: who the liaison served, what the account buys, that the Accountant exists, that the ledger exists, that the children's survival was falsified paperwork.
- He keeps the phone in a bowling bag in a Middle Park East locker he pays for in cash for ten years. This is the concealed evidence. It is concrete, portable, and it will be handed over only when Haru has earned it — or when Niko's guilt can no longer carry it.

## PART C — THE QUIET YEARS (2009–2019)

### C1. Haru abroad
- The heirs are settled in Europe under the attorneys' arrangements. Haru, 22 at the massacre, spends a decade building a legitimate logistics and marine-services firm — ports, manifests, customs rhythm. He is rich by 30, formidable by inclination and by the decade's hard school: he tracks the network's *peripheral* cells across two continents, and the name **SASIN** attaches to him in exactly those rooms — the man who arrives after the invoice does. The myth is real but regional; he has never once touched the center, because he has never had a thread to pull. **He confuses the control he exercises over these small rooms with certainty about the whole map. This is the flaw the campaign attacks.** *(1.13, 1.14 confirmed canon.)*
- Min-ji, separately, becomes a corporate strategist of the first rank — acquisitions, contracts, records, reputation. Her doctrine: **information control prevents catastrophe.** She and Haru disagree about this for ten years, politely, from different continents.

### C2. The trust and the tick **[P-4.6]**
- Tae-joon's will (drafted 2008, amended January 2009 — days before his death) places the **master ledger** in a testamentary trust, sealed until the tenth anniversary of his death, then delivering to his children jointly: *the truth about the family business, complete.*
- This is the "why now." In **January 2019** the trust's trustee initiates statutory notice to the heirs. The notice is a routine filing — and it is a tripwire. The Accountant, who audits everything, sees it. Baek, in the middle of a **succession war** inside his own house (his lieutenants are testing whether the old man's grip has slipped), needs the ledger destroyed before it can deliver. The Accountant needs it delivered — or needs someone else to make its delivery expensive enough to trade with. Both men reach for the same tool: **the surviving son.**

### C3. The nudges — one sender, three touches **[P-4.9, P-4.10]**
All of the following are sent or arranged by **the Accountant**. None come from the Beneficiary. None come from Jae-Ho (he is under watch and only half-aware any of it is happening).
- **Print #1** (February 2019): from the spotter's insurance roll, a photograph of the massacre — the suited liaison visible at the warehouse door — mailed anonymously to Haru abroad, with a typed line: *"You were told an accident. It was a contract. Ask Niko Bellic who stood at the door."* **This is why Haru returns.**
- **Print #2** (March 2019): a second print from the same roll, with a **different figure circled in grease pencil** (the crew's        driver/lieutenant), left at Jae-Ho's grave by the Accountant. Dual purpose: bait to see who visits (Haru, the network's watchers, both), and a message to Jae-Ho that the Accountant knows where to reach him.
- **The docks text** (arrival night, April 2019): unsigned SMS from a burner: **"THEY'RE WATCHING. MEET ME AT THE DOCKS. —A."** Haru does not go. He goes to Niko — the controllable choice. The Accountant recalibrates: the son is a controller; controllers need proof before contact.
- **The distorted call** (Mission 3): the Accountant, voice-changed, warns Haru that he is watched — "not just by us" — and threatens the weakest node (Roman's taxi business) to set the terms of the game. He never names the Beneficiary. He is steering, not confessing.

### C4. Position at campaign start (April 2019)
- **Haru**: back in LC, photograph in possession, reunion with Niko arranged. Knows: it was a contract; Niko was there; someone is steering him. Does not know: sender, Beneficiary, Accountant, ledger, trust.
- **Niko**: knows everything in B7. Guilt intact. Expects this day.
- **Min-ji**: in LC since 2017, running Seorin's successor holdings and the litigation wind-down. Received the same trust notice, filed it under "information to control." Does not know her father's final transaction.
- **Jae-Ho**: alive, hidden, watched, aging badly. Knows B3's first layer and B4. Does not know he is the Accountant's insurance.
- **Kostov**: alive, respected in the diaspora security trade; believes the 2009 job was a legitimately commissioned cleanup that went loose; knows a courier chain, a phrase-set, and a name he was told to forget.
- **The Accountant**: playing both ends — steering Haru at Baek, keeping Jae-Ho as the doomsday file, planning to hand the ledger's location to whoever is still standing at the end. His name and face are Act IV material.
- **Baek Seung-ho**: mid-succession-war; wants the trust's contents dead; monitors the son's return with the patience of a man who has buried faster sons than this.

## PART D — CAMPAIGN EVENT LINE (mission-level truth)

*(Mission scripts are the player-facing realization of this line. Numbers refer to campaign-outline.md.)*
- **M1 The Return**: canon as C3/C4. Haru does not go to the docks.
- **M2 Dead Drop**: cemetery by day. Print #2 is found *because the Accountant wants it found*; the sedan that circles is the network's watchers, also baited. Mandu appears with strictly bounded knowledge (see his profile: witnessed/inferred/bluff tags). Apartment search yields the Kostov name — Jae-Ho kept a courier's note, not a confession.
- **M3 Monitored**: the distorted call. Three routes yield **exclusive** intel + **exclusive** cost (consequence-tracker.md). No route names the Beneficiary.
- **M4 The Man at the Docks**: the Accountant, voice-masked, meets Haru on his own stage. He admits only: "I mailed the map. I need the knife." Haru's counter-surveillance is anticipated (lesson one: control ≠ certainty). Haru leaves with a torn ledger page — bait, gift, and hook in one.
- **M5–M8 (Family Empire)**: Min-ji's ledger-structure analysis coins the term **"the Beneficiary"** — every dirty trail terminates in an account that only ever *receives* **[P-4.10 payoff]**. The Photographer is found (M7); Roman's booking pattern exposes the watchers' rotation (M8); the subsidiary bid war exposes the succession war's LC front.
- **M9–M11 (The Man Who Lied)**: the sexton's ledger → the light coffin; Jae-Ho found; his confession (B3 layer one); the Accountant's fabrication is deduced (not confessed); **Niko's burner surfaces** — the rupture. The network takes Jae-Ho at act's end: the doomsday file is being repossessed.
- **M12–M14 (The Beneficiary)**: the account that clears Friday + Haeun's LC restructuring + the liaison's face (from print #1) resolve to **Baek Seung-ho** **[P-4.11 payoff]**. The parley: Baek offers Haru the network's chair — control as the price of silence. Baek's counterstroke (M14) hits Roman, Mandu, Jae-Ho simultaneously; the Accountant defects with the master ledger's location — which is itself the trap that was always meant to close the succession war.
- **M15–M17 (SASIN)**: the trust delivers (M15): the father's full record, including the final transaction that summoned the massacre. The Reckoning (M16–M17): Min-ji detonates the exposure package on her own terms; the network's partners, given certainty of Baek's exposure, remove him (the succession war eats its king); Haru, in the one room where killing is easy, chooses the certain outcome over the controlled one. Jae-Ho lives to face the family. Niko and Haru reach an uneasy accounting. Mandu, offered his price, declines it — the first person Baek ever met who did, and the detail that breaks him more than the exposure does. **[P-5.5]**

## PART F — CONTINUITY GUARDS (hard rules for every script)

- Kate: past tense, always. No line may imply the Deal ending occurred. Roman and Mallorie are married with a young daughter (named Kate, per P-1.18).
- Niko never says "the Beneficiary" or "the Accountant" before M12; his M3 recognition is of **phrases**, never of a face or voice.
- Mandu never states a fact he cannot source to witnessed/inferred/bluff/planted — scripts must be able to survive the question "how do you know that?"
- No character knows a fact before its clue exists in the schedule. If a line requires unknown knowledge, fix the line, not the schedule.
- Haru is hurt at least once per act; force never resolves a Min-ji problem, a records problem, or a reputation problem.
- Dates: massacre March 9, 2009; trust notice January 2019; return April 2019. The headstone says 2009.
